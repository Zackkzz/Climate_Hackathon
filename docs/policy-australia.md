# How a bill-neutral meter charge could work in Australia

*Policy note for Meterwise. Written 3 October 2026 from public sources. It is not legal advice; points marked
"to confirm" need a lawyer or the relevant regulator.*

## The short version

- The US model Meterwise borrows (Pay As You Save) relies on one utility that owns the wires, sells the power and
  sends the bill. Australia split those jobs apart, so the model cannot be copied directly.
- Three Australian routes can carry the same deal. The first is usable now; the other two need rule changes.
- The first route is community housing, where one organisation owns the block and funding already exists.

## 1. The barrier in Australian terms

- 30.6% of Australian households rent
  ([ABS 2021 Census](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/people-and-communities/snapshot-australia/latest-release)).
- The owner pays for hot water systems, heaters, insulation and the roof. The tenant pays the energy bills.
- In a strata block the roof is common property, so a cool roof needs the owners corporation to agree, and each flat
  may have a different owner.
- New rental standards will force some upgrades. Victoria's minimum energy efficiency standards phase in from
  1 March 2027 ([Consumer Affairs Victoria](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/legislation/public-consultations-and-reviews/new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards)).
  NSW consulted on its own standards in 2026
  ([NSW consultation paper](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/2026-04-DCCEEW-Consultation-Investigation-minimum-energy-efficiency-rental-standards.pdf));
  the final NSW dates are to confirm.
- Standards say what an owner must do. They do not provide the money. Meterwise is a way to pay for it.

## 2. What Pay As You Save needs, and what Australia has

| Pay As You Save needs | United States | Australia (National Electricity Market) |
|---|---|---|
| One body that invests and bills | Vertically integrated utility or co-operative | Network company and retailer are separate businesses |
| A charge tied to the meter, not the person | Utility tariff approved by a state commission | Network charges attach to the meter but pass through whichever retailer the tenant chooses |
| Authority to invest behind the meter | Granted in the tariff | Network companies are generally barred by ring-fencing rules unless the regulator grants a waiver ([AER example, 2024](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/networks/ring-fencing/ergon-energy-ring-fencing-waiver-behind-meter-august-2024)) |
| A charge that survives a change of occupant | Automatic | A retailer's charge ends when the tenant switches retailer |

US results show why the model is worth adapting:

- Cost recovery above 99.9%.
- Renters were a third to a half of participants at Ouachita Electric in Arkansas.
- 60–75% of households offered a deal accepted it, and about 90% where there was no upfront payment
  ([Clean Energy Works field review](https://www.cleanenergyworks.org/2022/10/25/field-experience-for-pays-in-the-us/)).

One caution from the same evidence: in Kansas, about half of participating households saved enough to cover their
charge ([Berkeley Lab review](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/customer-outcomes-pay-you-save)). Estimated savings
can miss. This is why Meterwise caps the charge below the modelled saving and why the pilot checks real bills.

## 3. Three delivery routes

### Route A: community housing provider (usable now)

- **Who pays:** the provider, using its own capital, a green loan or a grant.
- **Who repays:** the tenant, through an agreed service charge that is lower than the bill saving.
- **Why it works first:**
  - One owner for the whole block, so no strata vote.
  - Tenants pay their own energy bills, so the split incentive is real.
  - Money exists. The Social Housing Energy Performance Initiative is a $1.1 billion programme expected to reach
    more than 100,000 homes ([DCCEEW](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/energy/programs/social-housing)). NSW runs a
    Community Housing Energy Performance grant under it
    ([NSW](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/government-and-local-organisations/programs-grants-and-schemes/shepi/community-housing-energy)).
- **What Meterwise adds:** which blocks to do first, what package pays for itself, and how far a grant stretches when
  part of the cost is repaid from savings.
- **To confirm:** whether social housing rent rules allow a separate service charge, or whether the provider simply
  recovers nothing and treats the result as a bill cut for tenants. Meterwise shows both cases: set the tenant's
  share of savings to 100% and the funding gap becomes the grant needed.

### Route B: council rates charge (needs a NSW law change)

- **How it works:** a funder pays for the upgrade; the council collects repayments through a charge on the property's
  rates and passes them to the funder. The charge stays with the property when it is sold.
- **Precedents:**
  - NSW environmental upgrade agreements do this for commercial buildings and for strata residential buildings above
    20 lots ([NSW legislation](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/repealed/current/act-2010-110)).
  - Darebin council in Victoria paid for solar on almost 1,200 low-income homes and recovered the cost through rates
    over ten years, interest free
    ([Darebin](https://www.darebin.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/1/waste-environment-and-climate/documents/externalfaqsdarebinsolarsaverratesprogramdecember2023.pdf)).
    Very few private renters took part
    ([report](https://au.news.yahoo.com/one-local-council-helped-1-202606390.html)).
- **The gap:** a typical walk-up has 6 to 20 flats, below the 20-lot threshold. The blocks that most need this are
  the ones the law leaves out.
- **Changes needed:**
  1. Extend upgrade agreements to strata residential buildings of any size.
  2. Allow the owner to pass the charge to the tenant only up to a capped share of the modelled bill saving, with
     the tenant's written agreement. Commercial leases already allow pass-through; residential tenancy law limits
     what a landlord may charge (to confirm).
- **Strata approval is already easier:** since 2021 NSW owners corporations can approve sustainability upgrades to
  common property by simple majority
  ([summary](https://speirsryan.com.au/nsw-sustainable-strata-introduction-of-the-strata-schemes-management-amendment-sustainability-infrastructure-act-2021-and-what-it-means-for-strata-schemes/)).

### Route C: charge on the meter (needs national rule changes)

- **How it works:** the network company, or a funder working through it, pays for the upgrade. A fixed daily charge
  is added to that meter's network tariff. Every retailer must pass it through, so it survives a change of tenant or
  retailer.
- **Why it is the closest to Pay As You Save:** the charge follows the meter with no action from owner or tenant.
- **Changes needed:**
  1. A ring-fencing waiver or guideline change from the Australian Energy Regulator.
  2. A site-specific tariff component in the network pricing rules.
  3. Consumer protections written into the retail rules (below).
- **Timing:** network pricing is set in five-year periods, so this is a multi-year route.

### Why not put it on the retailer's bill?

Australian retailers do offer on-bill repayment for solar and batteries. The charge is a contract with the person,
not the meter. When the tenant moves out or switches retailer, the repayment stops or follows the person. That
breaks the deal, so Meterwise does not rely on it.

## 4. Protections the deal must carry

Whichever route is used, these are the conditions under which the deal is fair to the tenant. Meterwise's default
settings follow them.

| Protection | Rule | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| Bill neutrality | New bill plus charge is lower than the old bill | The charge is capped at 80% of the modelled saving |
| No debt for the tenant | The charge is tied to the property or meter and ends when the tenant leaves | Tenant upfront cost is always $0 |
| No disconnection for the charge | Arrears on the charge alone cannot cut off supply | A reserve (5% by default) is held against arrears |
| Equipment must work | The charge pauses while equipment is broken | Listed in the funder's sheet as a contract term |
| Disclosure | The next tenant is told about the charge before signing | Included in the owner's sheet |
| No rent rise for the upgrade | The owner paid nothing, so the upgrade is not grounds for a rent increase | Included in the owner's sheet (to confirm how this would be enforced) |

## 5. Who has to agree

| Party | What they are asked for | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant | Consent to the work and the charge | Lower total bill, cooler flat, hot water that costs less to run |
| Owner | Permission to install; in strata, a vote at a general meeting | New equipment at no cost, compliance with coming rental standards |
| Owners corporation | Simple-majority resolution for roof work | Roof coating at no cost to the sinking fund |
| Funder | Capital | Repayment with a return, measured emissions cuts |
| Council or housing provider | Administration | Progress on heat and emissions targets, lower tenant hardship |
| Installer | Performance warranty | A pipeline of similar jobs |

## 6. Existing programmes to combine with

- **Social Housing Energy Performance Initiative:** grant funding for social and community housing upgrades.
- **Household Energy Upgrades Fund:** $1 billion of discounted green loans through the Clean Energy Finance
  Corporation ([CEFC](https://www.cefc.com.au/where-we-invest/special-investment-programs/household-energy-upgrades-fund/)).
  A possible source of low-cost capital for the funder.
- **State certificate schemes:** discounts on heat pump hot water and efficient air conditioners. The amounts used
  in the app are listed, with sources, in the app's "What this assumes" panel.
- **Apartment solar grants:** NSW, Victoria and the ACT fund shared solar on apartment buildings. Solar is not in
  the first Meterwise package and is a natural addition.

## 7. Open questions

1. Can a NSW residential landlord lawfully pass on a rates-based upgrade charge with the tenant's consent?
2. Is a meter or rates charge "credit" under the National Credit Code? US programmes are designed to avoid being
   consumer loans; the Australian position is untested.
3. How do income-based social housing rents treat a service charge?
4. Who owns the equipment during and after the term, and who insures it?
5. What happens to the charge if the building is redeveloped before the term ends?

## 8. Link to COP31

- **Electrification:** the package replaces gas hot water, heating and cooking with electric equipment in homes
  that otherwise wait longest.
- **Resilient cities and buildings:** cool roofs and efficient cooling cut heat exposure and energy use in the
  hottest neighbourhoods.
- The brief asks for solutions that make climate action easier to finance and that turn climate-risk data into city
  action. Meterwise uses satellite heat data to choose buildings and a repayment model to pay for them.
